#### Entity Relationship Diagram

The Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD) shown in Figure 14 was developed to represent the data entities of the Web-Based AI-Assisted Ordinance Information System (Cabanatuan City Ordinance Portal) and the relationships established among them within the relational database. The design included nine entities: users, barangays, ordinances, ordinance_tags, reports, news_items, chat_sessions, chat_messages, and system_settings. Primary and foreign keys were used to establish the relationships necessary to support account authentication, role-based access control (RBAC: CITIZEN, BARANGAY_ADMIN, and LGU_ADMIN), geopolitical barangay jurisdiction routing, legislative ordinance drafting and Sanggunian resolution lifecycle management (DRAFT, PENDING, APPROVED, and REJECTED), multi-page scanned legal PDF document archiving, AI OCR document parsing, citizen community violation complaint submission and administrative triage, civic news and public advisory broadcasting, and bilingual (Tagalog/English) AI ordinance consultation powered by Google Gemini via Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

The ordinances entity served as the principal transactional and repository record for legislative operations. It maintained the official title, resolution number, ordinance classification label, legislative series year, jurisdiction scope (BARANGAY or CITY), statutory lifecycle status (DRAFT, PENDING, APPROVED, or REJECTED), full statutory transcript, structured article breakdown, prescribed penal sanctions, signatories, official scanned PDF file path, enactment date, calendar year, coverage boundary, and designated implementing enforcement agencies. Its relationships enforce rigorous administrative accountability through two foreign keys referencing the users entity: submitted_by_id identifies the local official who authored or encoded the enactment, while reviewed_by_id identifies the LGU administrator responsible for statutory review, approval, or formal rejection (with corresponding administrative feedback recorded in rejected_reason). The ordinances entity is also linked to barangays via barangay_id, which designates the enacting local barangay council for localized ordinances or remains null for city-wide statutes that apply across all 89 barangays of Cabanatuan City. Supporting records were linked to an ordinance through ordinance_tags to enable multi-field weighted heuristic scoring (10 for Title, 8 for Tags, 6 for Category, 4 for Description, 3 for Articles, 3 for Penalties, and 2 for Content) during RAG vector context retrieval.

The barangays entity maintained one-to-many relationships with users, ordinances, and reports, establishing the geopolitical foundation of the portal. It links to users to delineate administrative jurisdiction for Barangay Administrators and residence for registered citizens, to ordinances to organize localized legislative archives, and to reports to direct community violation tickets directly to the concerned barangay hall. The reports entity enabled residents to lodge localized violation complaints across defined categories (TRASH_BURNING, NOISE, ROAD_OBSTRUCTION, and OTHER) with uploaded photographic evidence and optional contact verification (contact_name and contact_phone); it incorporates an is_anonymous boolean flag to safeguard citizen whistleblower privacy by permitting unauthenticated or anonymized submissions while retaining administrative status triage (NEW, IN_PROGRESS, RESOLVED, and DISMISSED). The chat_sessions and chat_messages entities captured the multi-turn conversational interactions between citizens and the AI legal assistant. chat_sessions maintained a nullable foreign key to users (user_id), allowing unauthenticated guest citizens to consult local ordinances with zero onboarding friction while permitting registered users to retain multi-turn dialogue histories. Each session contained one or more chat_messages capturing the interlocutor role (user or assistant), prompt queries, cited statutory resolution numbers, and timestamps. The news_items entity disseminated public advisories categorized by territorial scope (CITY or BARANGAY) with pinning capabilities (is_pinned). Finally, the system_settings entity stored runtime configuration parameters—such as the RAG top-k threshold, similarity cutoffs, Gemini model temperature, and file upload size limits—as dynamic key-value records.

Overall, the ERD established the structural organization of the principal records required by the Cabanatuan City Ordinance Portal. It provided the basis for implementing relationships among system data while supporting referential consistency, cascade constraints, and reducing unnecessary duplication. The logical data structure was further refined through the database normalization process from Unnormalized Form to Third Normal Form.

#### Database Normalization

Following the conceptual and logical schema defined in the Entity Relationship Diagram (Figure 14), database normalization was performed to organize the logical data structure of the Web-Based AI-Assisted Ordinance Information System (Cabanatuan City Ordinance Portal), reduce unnecessary data redundancy, and establish appropriate dependencies among related civic and legal data elements. The normalization process documented the progressive refinement of the database structure from Unnormalized Form (UNF) to First Normal Form (1NF), Second Normal Form (2NF), and Third Normal Form (3NF).

The UNF (Figure 15) represented the initial broad data structure in which related and repeating governance information—such as user credentials, administrative role privileges, barangay geopolitical jurisdictions, legislative ordinance records, statutory articles, penalty clauses, uploaded scanned PDF copies, semantic tags, citizen community reports, civic news announcements, AI chat consultation sessions, conversational dialogue messages, and system parameters—had not yet been separated into normalized relations. During 1NF (Figure 16), repeating groups were eliminated, data elements were organized into atomic values, and each relation was given a primary key, utilizing composite primary keys for child relations such as ordinance tags and chat messages. The 2NF stage (Figure 17) removed partial functional dependencies: individual chat messages and ordinance tags were keyed by their own dedicated identifiers, citizen community reports and civic news bulletins were decoupled into independent relations, and user authentication records and barangay profiles were separated from legislative transaction tables. In 3NF (Figure 18), remaining transitive dependencies were eliminated by removing barangay, submitter, reviewer, reporter, and user names that depended on referenced foreign keys rather than on the primary key, leaving the nine relations of the implemented schema (`users`, `barangays`, `ordinances`, `ordinance_tags`, `reports`, `news_items`, `chat_sessions`, `chat_messages`, and `system_settings`), which correspond directly to the entities modeled in the ERD. Contact details in community reports were intentionally retained within the reports relation to support optional citizen anonymity while preserving contact verification without enforcing mandatory user account registration.

The resulting normalized structure provided a dependable basis for maintaining related records, eliminating data anomalies (insertion, update, and deletion anomalies), and ensuring high transaction throughput across the web portal and the Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) AI assistant. The normalized relations subsequently served as the basis for the Data Dictionary, which documented the field-level structure of the database.
