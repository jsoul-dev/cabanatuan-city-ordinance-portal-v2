#### Entity Relationship Diagram

The Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD) shown in Figure 14 was developed to represent the data entities of the Web-Based AI-Assisted Ordinance Information System (Cabanatuan City Ordinance Portal) and the relationships established among them within the relational database. The design included six primary entities corresponding to the implemented database schema and Data Dictionary: users, barangays, ordinances, reports, news items (news_items), and chat sessions (chat_sessions). Primary and foreign keys were used to establish the relationships necessary to support account authentication, role-based access control (RBAC: CITIZEN, BARANGAY_ADMIN, and LGU_ADMIN), geopolitical barangay jurisdiction routing, legislative ordinance drafting and Sanggunian resolution lifecycle management (DRAFT, PENDING, APPROVED, and REJECTED), multi-page scanned legal PDF document archiving, AI OCR document parsing, citizen community violation complaint submission and administrative triage, civic news and public advisory broadcasting, and bilingual (Tagalog/English) AI ordinance consultation powered by Google Gemini via Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

The ordinances entity served as the principal transactional and repository record for legislative operations. It maintained the official title, resolution number, ordinance classification label, legislative series year, jurisdiction scope (BARANGAY or CITY), statutory lifecycle status (DRAFT, PENDING, APPROVED, or REJECTED), full statutory transcript, structured article breakdown, prescribed penal sanctions, signatories, official scanned PDF file path, enactment date, calendar year, coverage boundary, and designated implementing enforcement agencies. Curated semantic keywords used for weighted heuristic retrieval in the RAG search pipeline were stored directly within the structured tags JSON attribute. The entity's relationships enforce rigorous administrative accountability through two foreign keys referencing the users entity: submitted_by_id identifies the local official who authored or encoded the enactment, while reviewed_by_id identifies the LGU administrator responsible for statutory review, approval, or formal rejection (with corresponding administrative feedback recorded in rejected_reason). The ordinances entity is also linked to barangays via barangay_id, which designates the enacting local barangay council for localized ordinances or remains null for city-wide statutes that apply across all 89 barangays of Cabanatuan City.

The barangays entity maintained one-to-many relationships with users, ordinances, and reports, establishing the geopolitical foundation of the portal. It links to users to delineate administrative jurisdiction for Barangay Administrators and residence for registered citizens, to ordinances to organize localized legislative archives, and to reports to direct community violation tickets directly to the concerned barangay hall. The reports entity enabled residents to lodge localized violation complaints across defined categories (TRASH_BURNING, NOISE, ROAD_OBSTRUCTION, and OTHER) with uploaded photographic evidence and optional contact verification (contact_name and contact_phone); it incorporates an is_anonymous boolean flag to safeguard citizen whistleblower privacy by permitting unauthenticated or anonymized submissions while retaining administrative status triage (NEW, IN_PROGRESS, RESOLVED, and DISMISSED). The chat sessions entity captured the multi-turn conversational interactions between citizens and the AI legal assistant, storing dialogue turns (user queries and AI-grounded responses) as structured JSON in the messages attribute; it maintained a nullable foreign key to users (user_id), allowing unauthenticated guest citizens to consult local ordinances with zero onboarding friction while permitting registered users to retain multi-turn dialogue histories. Finally, the news items entity disseminated public advisories categorized by territorial scope (CITY or BARANGAY) with pinning capabilities (is_pinned).

Overall, the ERD established the structural organization of the principal records required by the Cabanatuan City Ordinance Portal. It provided the basis for implementing relationships among system data while supporting referential consistency, cascade constraints, and reducing unnecessary duplication. The logical data structure was further analyzed and validated through the database normalization process from Unnormalized Form to Third Normal Form.
